Instrument History Explorer

An application for learning about the history of musical instruments.

Topic Description

The topic domain is on the history of musical instruments. Instruments tend to have incredibly varied origins, with some instruments being thousands of years old and others being invented in the last century. Many often carry some cultural significance, and have some storied history. With an incredibly vast amount of instruments, with different classifications, origins, and histories, there is a truly large amount of information to explore.

What drove me to choose this topic is my personal interest in music and musical instruments. In middle school and highschool, I played a variety of percussion instruments, including the Glockenspiel, Marimba, Vibraphone, Timpani, Bass Drum, Snare Drum, and more. I found it endlessly fascinating to learn about the history of these instruments, and how different cultures and regions of the world have developed their own unique, yet strikingly similar instruments. This also comes a little personally, as I felt like I would always have to explain to people what a Marimba or Vibraphone was, and I wanted to be able to provide a resource for people to learn about these instruments and their history.

There isn't necessarily a single "one-stop" resource for learning about the history of musical instruments. The closest thing to that are resources like Wikipedia, Encyclopedia Britannica, and other similar resources. However, these resources are not necessarily focused on musical instruments, and they are not necessarily organized in a way that supports it.

Application Description

Instrument History Explorer is a proposed educational web application designed to help users learn about the history and development of musical instruments. The application will organize instruments into four families: percussion, woodwinds, brass, and strings. Users will explore the topic through a sequence of selections, ultimately leading to detailed information about a specific instrument.

Home Page

The first page will be the landing page. It will introduce the purpose of the application and explain how learning about instruments can provide insight into music, culture, and history. The page will include a welcome message, a short explanation of the four instrument families, a featured instrument image, and navigation to the other application pages.

Instrument Explorer Page

The second page will contain the main interactive feature. It will use two steps that occur in sequence. First, the user will select one of four instrument families. After that selection, the application will display four instruments belonging to the selected family. The user will then select one of those instruments to view its information.

  1. Step 1: Select one instrument family: percussion, woodwinds, brass, or strings.
  2. Step 2: Select one instrument from the four choices displayed for the selected family.
  3. Result: The application will display information based on both selections.

The second step depends on the first step. For example, selecting “Brass” will display only trumpet, trombone, tuba, and euphonium. The user will not see the woodwind or string instruments unless they return to the first step and select a different family. In the future, JavaScript will respond to each selection and change the information displayed on the page.

Instrument Selection Data

  • Percussion: Snare Drum, Bongos, Marimba, Piano
  • Woodwinds: Flute, Saxophone, Bassoon, Clarinet
  • Brass: Tuba, Trumpet, Trombone, Euphonium
  • Strings: Violin, Harp, Shamisen, Guitar

This creates four first-step choices and four second-step choices for each family, resulting in 16 possible instrument selections.

Final Information for Each Instrument

Every one of the 16 instruments will use the same type of final information. After the user makes both selections, the application will display the instrument's name, family, geographic or cultural origins, historical timeline, description of how it produces sound, and its role in music. Each instrument will also include two images, three historical facts, and links to reliable sources.

Music History Page

The third page will provide additional information for users to read unrelated to the rest of the application. It will discuss how instruments are classified, how musical instruments have changed over time, the relationship between instruments and different cultures, basic music theory, and the development of musical genres. This page will provide broader background information rather than repeating the individual instrument information from the explorer page.

Contact Us Page

The fourth page will be a Contact Us page. It will contain a form for collecting the user's name, email address, subject, and message. Users may use the form to suggest an instrument, ask a question, or report incorrect information.

Resources and Image Permissions

Research will use reliable music history resources, including the following:

  • Smithsonian Music
  • Library of Congress: Notated Music
  • Wikimedia Commons
  • Encyclopedia Britannica: Musical Instruments

Images will be obtained from public-domain or Creative Commons sources. Each image will include attribution and a link to its license when required.
